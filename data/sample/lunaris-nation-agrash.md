**Agrash Alliance**  
*Tradition and Honor*  
The North Western Isles  
*(For the purposes of the Agrash section, any mentions of ‘Her’ refers to Ilneva Herself)*

Agrash is a land of harsh contrasts and unyielding resolve, an archipelago where survival is a testament to the endurance, resilience, and camaraderie of its people. Scattered across five distinct islands, each embodying a unique virtue, Agrash stands as a bastion of honor and unity in a world fraught with danger. The islands are shaped by the relentless forces of nature, where the primal and corrupted elements threaten to overwhelm the inhabitants at every turn. The people of Agrash, bound by blood and a code of mutual respect, eschew currency in favor of service to one another, valuing honor above all else. Their society is led by the Lash Ilneva, those who have fought and embodied each virtue well to deserve the title of Her flesh. Despite the constant challenges posed by untamed wilderness known to them as the Verit Resht and the creeping blight of the Wilted Face known to the as the Kavdo, Agrash thrives through sheer will and the unwavering dedication of its people, who see each day as a battle to preserve their land, their honor, and their way of life.

### **Society and Governance**

The Lash Ilneva (Ilneva in Flesh) stands at the heart of Agrash, for it is their duty to hold the islands together as one alliance. In a land where survival depends on unity, the Lash Ilneva works closely with the Syrvaks, the councils devoted to preserving the ancient virtues of Ilneva, to maintain the fragile balance that binds Agrash into a single people. Their leadership is not absolute but is measured against their honor within the alliance. The Lash Ilneva is never questioned as all Agrashans have respected and believed in the might and wisdom of each one so far. The holy texts allow for the derogation of the status of Lash Ilneva from an Agrashan through the unified voice of the islands but that has yet to happen. 

Beneath the Lash Ilneva and the Syrvaks are the Kitral, elders who have chosen to forgo the Rite of Release, when the people request that they stay and provide their wisdom for their island. Each island keeps several Kitral at a time, not as rulers, but as guides. They are the stewards of tradition: organizing Brakkas where the people gather to decide the direction of the island, arranging feasts to honor Her or the fallen, and serving as arbiters in lesser disputes that do not warrant judgment from the Syrvak Tirim.

It is within the Brakka that the will of the people is heard. Anyone may speak, and the loudest voices can sway the course of the island, though the Kitral ensures order is kept and the proceedings endure until a decision is reached. Should a Clamor drag on for too long, the Kitral are expected to push the matter toward resolution, lest discord weaken the island’s honor. In this way, governance in Agrash is neither wholly the work of leaders nor wholly the voice of the people.

#### **Kaar Nev (Her Rising)**

*Nev is an honorific pronoun meaning Her, specifically used to refer to Ilneva. Kaar means birth or arrival.* 

Every six years, on the last full moon of the year, a crimson moon rises over Lunaris. On this night, the greatest trial of Agrash is held: the Kaar Nev, meaning Her Rising, for it is believed that Ilneva herself ascends once more through the victor. One champion from each island enters mortal combat.

The survivor of this rite inherits the fallen opponent’s virtue, carrying the weight of every island they defeat. The final victor is named Lash Ilneva meaning “Ilneva in the Flesh” for they embody all her virtues until death claims them. From that moment onwards, they rule the alliance, their word binding all of Agrash.

If the Lash Ilneva comes to believe they do not possess the strength to endure until the next Kaar Nev, they may request their Drumkhar. In such cases, they remain in power until their successor is chosen at the following rite. Only then, once the new Lash Ilneva has risen, do they meet their end at the successor’s hand. To be slain in one’s Drumkhar by the Lash Ilneva is regarded as the highest honor, a final act of service that binds one’s name forever to Ilneva’s legacy. 

Should the Lash Ilneva perish before the appointed six years, the rite is held again on the next full moon, crimson or not, for the alliance cannot linger leaderless.

As a mark of their unique station, a Lash Ilneva can never become a Kitral; their role in life and death is set apart from all others.

#### **Brakka**

The Brakka is the great gathering of the people, where the voice of the island is heard in full. Anyone may attend, and anyone with the strength to raise their voice above the crowd may speak. Shouts, chants, and the pounding of feet or fists give weight to an argument, for in Agrash passion is as much proof as reason. When individuals of great honor or renown enter the Brakka it is common to hear silence from all attendees to let such individuals speak unimpeded. 

The Kitral serve as stewards of the Brakka, calling it when decisions must be made, ensuring order is kept, and pressing disputes forward when debate grows too long. Though the Brakka can stretch across many days, the Kitral will not allow it to linger past a full turning of the moon, for indecision is dishonor. In this way, the Brakka ensures that no voice is left unheard, yet no island is left rudderless.

### **Agrashan Upbringing**

From birth, all children of Agrash are raised on the island of Umra, where they are immersed in the shared traditions, values, and wisdom of the alliance. At the age of eight, they are given the choice of which island they will devote their lives to, a decision guided by the signs read by the Syrvak Rinor (Council of Signs) and their personal inclinations. Once chosen, they leave Umra to live and train among the people of their selected island, preparing for the role they aim to fulfill.

At the age of fifteen, they face a trial unique to each island's virtue, testing whether they are ready to contribute as capable and honorable members of society. Those who fail are returned to Ilneva ensuring that their spirit resides in honor, freeing them from a life that is too much for them to bear. Agrashans believe that children who are sacrificed back to Ilneva are permitted to live with her among the stars and the moon even though they never became true servants of hers. This act, though solemn and tragic, is regarded as a way of giving mercy to these children: allowing them to live with Ilneva even with their shortcomings and freeing them from these tortuous lands.

### **The Islands and their Virtues**

**Shavtal**  
*Virtue: Dedication and Endurance* 

The people of Shavtal have adapted to these harsh conditions, cultivating a society where every task is seen as a test of endurance. Their daily lives are marked by a relentless work ethic, where overcoming physical and spiritual trials is a part of their identity. Dedication to one's duties and the ability to endure life's challenges are the highest virtues on this island, where survival is a continuous struggle against the elements.

**Duraak**  
*Virtue: Justice and Discipline* 

Life on Duraak demands strict order and self-control, with every inhabitant trained to withstand the island's natural challenges. Discipline is not just a virtue here; it's a way of life, with the people of Duraak taking pride in their ability to maintain composure and strength, no matter the adversity they face.

**Kashka**  
*Virtue: Strategy and Leadership* 

The inhabitants of Kashka are known for their tactical acumen, excelling in both military and political arenas. The island's society values foresight and calculated decision-making, with its people believing that true leadership is demonstrated by the ability to anticipate challenges and guide others through them. In Kashka, strategy is not merely a skill, it's an essential component of survival and success.

**Kulash**  
*Virtue: Camaraderie and Courage* 

The inhabitants of Kulash are deeply bonded by their shared experiences and mutual support, facing challenges together with bravery and a collective spirit. Courage here is seen not just as individual valor but as the strength that comes from standing together as a community. The people of Kulash believe that true courage is best demonstrated when they face adversity side by side, united in purpose and resolve.

**~~Tharak~~**   
*Virtue: Magical Mastery and Dominion*

Tharak was once the island of the arcane and occult, where orcs studied magic to wield against the veil and the enemies of the alliance. Its people delved deep into the stars and secrets of power, believing sorcery to be their shield and strength or so it’s told. The Verit Resht and Kavdo converged on Tharak and the island was consumed, never to be seen again. The orcs saw this as Ilneva’s sign that this moniker of theirs shall not be followed any longer, and so no magic shall be studied again. Only the rites and auguries of Umra and the clerics of Ilneva remain sacred, for all other magic is a curse.

**Umra**  
*Virtue: Tradition and Ceremonies*

The people of Umra, the elders and clerics that populate the island, are deeply connected to their past, preserving and honoring their traditions through elaborate ceremonies and rites. These practices are seen as essential to maintaining the island's identity, with the belief that by upholding their ancestors' ways, they are safeguarding the wisdom and prosperity of future generations. In Umra, tradition is not just respected; it is revered as the cornerstone of their society.

### **Syrvak (Councils)**

Each island has its own specialized council focusing on the virtue they represent. These councils are the medium in which the alliance 

**Syrvak Moren (Council of Instructors)**  
Location: Shavtal  
The Syrvak Moren upholds the virtues of dedication and endurance by overseeing the creation and continuance of physical and spiritual trials. This council is responsible for training warriors, testing leaders, and ensuring the people of Agrash remain resilient. These trials are seen as rites of passage, forging the strength needed to endure the harsh realities of Agrash. The people of Shavtal view Syrvak Moren as the heart of their identity, believing that through pain and perseverance, greatness is achieved.

**Syrvak Tirim (Council of Statute)**  
Location: Duraak  
The Syrvak Tirim act as the arbiters of Agrash, resolving conflicts between large communities or between the people of islands. This council is revered for its fairness, with members chosen for their wisdom, patience, and deep understanding of Agrash traditions. Their rulings are final, and their goal is to uphold unity in the alliance by ensuring disputes are settled with justice and respect for all involved.

**Syrvak Arsha (Council of Tactics)**  
Location: Kashka  
The Syrvak Arsha is the backbone of Agrash's military strength. This council crafts and updates defense strategies, coordinates troop formations, and prepares contingency plans for threats to the islands. Composed of master tacticians and seasoned warriors, they ensure that no island is left unprotected. Their decisions are vital for maintaining Agrash’s readiness against enemies, whether they arise from within or beyond.

**Syrvak Veyir (Council of Comradeship)**  
Location: Kulash  
The Syrvak Veyir is tasked with overseeing the trade networks and ensuring inter-island cooperation thrives. They coordinate resource exchanges and maintain the balance of wealth and goods across the islands. 

**Syrvak Rinor (Council of Omens)**  
Location: Umra  
The Syrvak Rinor serves as Agrash’s spiritual guides, reading the stars, weather patterns, and other natural signs to predict challenges and opportunities. Their interpretations are considered messages from the land, ancestors, and even from Ilneva herself. The people of Umra revere this council as protectors of tradition, tying their future to the wisdom of the past.

### **Asha (Marks of Honor)**

Ashas are marks of honors granted to each Agrashan after the age of 15\. These marks tell the story of how each person has given to the alliance, whether through strength, craft, or sustenance. These are not to be worn lightly only at brakkas, weddings, funerals, and other sacred gatherings.

Ashas are not worn lightly. They are sacred symbols of service and are displayed only on solemn or communal occasions: alliance gatherings, weddings, funerals, feasts, and war-councils. To wear them every day is seen as flaunting honor rather than respecting it.

**Types of Ashas**

**Warriors**

Warriors wear cloaks or mantles fashioned from the hides of the greatest beast they have slain. A hide is a trophy, proof of their strength and courage. Ashas can be made out of any hide seen by that warrior as worthy to show their honor: a great beast’s scales or hide, a cloak decorated with bones from the Kavdo, the armor of a formidable enemy of Agrash, all of these are suitable ashas. 

A warrior’s cloak is more than a display of victories. When a warrior owes their life to another: whether to a healer who mended their wounds, or a smith whose blade won the day, they tear a strip from their cloak and offer it as a gift. In this way, their triumphs are shared, and their honor binds to others.

Warriors are expected to eventually replace their mantle with a comparable or stronger beast. A warrior who bears a short ragged cloak, torn thin from too much thanking, is stigmatized. People assume they were saved more often than they slew, and thus are seen as weak or useless. To bear a mighty hide that remains long is a sign of independence, skill, and continuing power.

**Teachers, Crafters, or Healers**

Those who serve not by slaying beasts but by mending, forging, or teaching wear a simple metal necklace, a bare chain or a simple ring of metal. Over time, this chain becomes decorated with the gratitude of warriors: scraps of hide tied to it, each one a piece of a cloak given in thanks.

The more decorated the chain, the more lives this orc has strengthened. A smith with a chain covered in wolf fur and serpent scale is known to have armed many warriors. A healer with dozens of hide scraps on their necklace is proof of one who has saved countless lives.

**Providers** 

The work of farmers and fishers is not marked by single great deeds, but by constant endurance. They wear armbands or wristbands woven from cord, reed, or sinew. Each season that yields harvest or catch, new knots, beads, or shells are added.

During feasts, warriors and Kitral or Syrvaks publicly acknowledge the providers, giving them carved bone or small metal tokens to add to their bands. A farmer with many-layered bands is as honored as a warrior with the mightiest cloak.

**Purposeful Breaking or Damaging Ashas**

**Respectful Breaking**

An Agrasahan who tears their marks in half before their kin or allies does so to acknowledge they can no longer give. This is the signal that they ask for the drumkhar: a final, honorable death to spare the alliance from their weakness.

**Disrespectful Breaking**

To rip one’s marks in anger or scorn is to reject the islands and the alliance. This act severs them from Agrash, casting them out as deserters.

**Breaking Another’s Symbol**

To seize and tear another’s marks is to question their place within the alliance. Such an accusation cannot be ignored.

* If the accused is a warrior, they must duel the accuser to prove their honor.  
* If the accused is not a warrior, they may name a champion, any willing warrior, to fight on their behalf.  
* The duel decides the truth: if the accused (or their champion) wins, their honor is upheld and the accusation dismissed. If they lose, their shame is proven, and the consequences fall swiftly.

### **The Verit Resht & Kavdo**

The people of Agrash find themselves caught between the hammer of nature’s primal fury and the anvil of its darkest corruption, fighting a losing battle to hold onto their lands, their lives, and their very souls. Those who face the Verit Resht must live as nomads, constantly moving to avoid its creeping dominion, while those who battle the Kavdo fortify their civilizations into bastions, determined to halt the undead scourge from encroaching upon their homes. The Verit Resht never fully consumes an island; its wrath flows like a tide, swelling in one region and receding in another, offering brief, fragile havens. Conversely, the Kavdo's black corruption is relentless, spreading its blight across the land without reprieve and forcing its defenders into a grim, perpetual war of attrition.

**Verit Resht**

In the heart of Agrash, a sinister and unstoppable spread of crimson flora, known to the locals as *Verit Resht* or the ‘*Red Rage*’ in nomadic, has taken root. This red foliage, akin to nature on a brutal surge of power, infuses the land with an overwhelming primal energy. The plants grow taller and thicker, their roots sinking deep into the soil as if consuming the very essence of the earth. Animals and creatures within these areas are affected similarly, they grow larger, stronger, and far more aggressive, driven by an unquenchable thirst for dominance. Even the smallest of creatures become deadly predators, and once peaceful beasts turn into terrifying, relentless hunters.

The land itself seems to breathe with this savage energy, pulsating with an unnatural vitality that warps everything within its grasp. As if this weren't dire enough, the *Verit Resht* heralds the presence of an ancient and unkillable beast, known as *Reshtil*, which roams these lands. This creature, towering like a mountain, is a living embodiment of nature's wrath. Countless attempts to slay *Reshtil* have all ended in failure, with entire armies crushed underfoot or scattered by its earth-shaking roars. The red lands of Agrash are a place where survival is a constant battle, and where the natural order has been upended by a force beyond mortal control.

**Kavdo**

In contrast to the seething vitality of the *Verit Resht*, another, more insidious threat has begun to corrupt the lands of Agrash. Known as the *Kavdo* or the ‘*undeath*’ in nomadic, this creeping black blight first appeared after the Fracturing, spreading like a cancer through the fertile lands. The Wilted Face corrupts everything it touches: rivers and lakes turn to thick, black ichor, undrinkable and deadly to all life. Animals, once innocent or even majestic, become twisted monstrosities, their bodies warped and their minds driven mad. Food harvested from these lands is tainted, turning to poison in the mouths of those who consume it, while the very soil itself becomes a breeding ground for disease and decay.

Perhaps most terrifyingly, the dead do not rest in lands claimed by the Wilted Face. Corpses rise animated by the black corruption that twists them into grotesque undead abominations. These walking dead are drawn to the living, hungering for the life that was taken from them, and spreading the blight with every cursed step they take.

### **Worship**

Ilneva, the goddess of Agrash, stands as the eternal protector and paragon of the virtues that define her people. She is not merely a deity but a living force, woven into the very fabric of the islands and the hearts of her followers. Her story is one of unparalleled strength, unyielding resolve, and boundless love for her land and people. From her youth, Ilneva fought against the mist that encroaches the world, driving back the primal forces that sought to consume it. It is said that every mile of fertile land, every safe harbor, and every breath of peace that Lunaris knows today was wrested from chaos by her hands.

Her life is divided into the virtues that now guide Agrashan society. As Shavtal, she endured unimaginable trials, proving that perseverance could outlast even the harshest of fates. As Duraak, she mastered discipline and resilience, crafting the backbone of Agrash’s spirit. As Kulash, she stood as a unifying force, leading warriors into battle with courage born of camaraderie. As Kashka, she outwitted beasts and the spirits of nature, her strategic brilliance paving the way for a thriving civilization. As Tharak, she delved into the mysteries of magic, seeking mastery over the unseen forces that shaped the world. Finally, as Umra, she wove together the traditions and ceremonies that ensure Agrash's identity endures. In death, her blood ascended to the moon, her essence becoming one with the celestial bodies, imbuing Lunaris with her divine strength. Her light, reflected in the night sky, serves as a constant reminder of her guardianship.

### **Drumkhar (The Death of my Strength)** 

*Khar means death or departure and Druma is a philosophical concept used to describe all the effort a person has in their lifetime.* 

There comes a time when even the strongest warrior or the most diligent worker can no longer give. When they have expended their druma to its emptiness. To linger and drain the Isles’ strength is the greatest shame, so many orcs, when they feel their usefulness has ended, ask for what is known as a drumkhar. An Agrashan must deliberately rend or break their asha in peace in front of any of their kin or allies to ask for drumkhar. If one does not have their asha in their times of weakness, a symbolic piece similar to their asha is destroyed in its place. 

Drumkhar is an honorable death, chosen freely, to spare the clan from the burden of their weakness. Warriors typically spar one last time to show that their spirit has not faltered, even if their body has. When the spar is done, they are executed swiftly by a chosen person. It is common to choose a spouse, the strongest member of your kin, or an ally you have fought alongside with. Those who do not wish to spar are given an easy death without struggle, a common choice for those who were not raised as warriors but as crafters, farmers, or priests.

Those who die in their sleep or to organ failure (as long as they were still in service of the alliance) are seen as having gone through drumkhar.
