# **Lunaris🌙**

In the Quill of Infinites lies the realm of Lunaris, a world where the very essences of reality clash and tear at one another in perpetual conflict. Lunarians are not born under the fortune of stars or the grace of gods; they are born into a world where luck is an alien concept. Life in Lunaris is a relentless struggle, a grim existence defined by the harsh forces of nature and magic that pervade every corner of the land. Survival demands strict societal controls, deep-seated superstitions, and an uneasy balance against the ever-present threat of monstrous beings. In this realm, even the gods seem to have abandoned their dominion, leaving mortals to fend for themselves in a world that offers no mercy.  
	

## **Nature**

| *"The nature of the world cares not for our lives, cities, or the children that we bear. We are mere embers in its vast storm. Respect it or, if you must, fear it, for it will not hesitate to snuff you out." — Thalor of House Croifort, Warden of the Whitecloaks* |
| :---- |

Nature in Lunaris is a force of immense power, one that is as unforgiving as it is integral. It is the strongest force of magic in Lunaris, all civilizations have accepted this as fact (except for Zaldera1). In this world, nature is not a gentle provider but a relentless force that shapes the lives of those who dwell within its grasp. The forests are deep and ancient, the mountains high and unyielding, and the rivers swift and untamed. Every element of the natural world carries a latent threat, a reminder that in Lunaris, survival is a daily struggle. 

The **Primal Face** of nature is the embodiment of life’s raw, untamed power. It is the force that drives the cycle of life and death, growth and decay, with a fierce intensity. The primal forests are dense and teeming with life, but that life is wild and unpredictable. The beasts are strong, cunning, and always hungry; the plants grow thick and fast, often possessing thorns, toxins, or carnivorous tendencies. Druids and those who revere nature draw power from this face, but they know that it must be respected, for the Primal Face is not merciful. It is the part of nature that reminds mortals of their fragility, where even a moment’s inattention can mean death. Civilizations form their own way to view the powers of the primal face, some see it as a face that needs to be worshiped and appeased, some see it as the direct adversary to the progress of civilization: creating counters and measures to protect themself from it, some see it as a face to be avoided with superstitious beliefs and ritualistic practices, and for some it’s a face to live with harmoniously as a protector. 

The **Wilted Face** represents the corruption and decay that lies beneath nature’s surface, where life has been twisted into something grotesque. This face is the darker, more sinister side of the natural world, where the once vibrant becomes warped and malevolent. Here, the forests are not just dangerous, but actively hostile. Trees might grasp at passersby with thorny vines, and animals are monstrous aberrations, driven mad by the corruption within them. The Wilted Face is the shadow of the Primal, a reminder that all life can be perverted and that the line between life and death, growth and decay, is often disturbingly thin. In Lunaris, this face haunts the land, a constant threat of nature turning against itself, and against those who dare to live within it.

## **Monsters**

| *"We are never truly alone. The shadows of the trees, the rustle in the underbrush, they are the whispers of beasts waiting to claim the unwary and unwise. Keep your blade sharp and your wits sharper."* — *Komarod of Kulash, Lash Ilneva (Late)* |
| :---- |

Monsters are an ever-present reality in Lunaris, appearing in what seems to be endless numbers. These creatures of nature lurk in every uncivilized corner of the realm, making it perilous to venture beyond the safety of cities and towns. It is unwise for anyone to set foot outside of civilization without a means of offense, defense, or stealth; ideally, a combination of all three. Without such precautions, you are nothing more than prey in the wilds. To instill caution in the young, the elderly and parental figures share horror stories, warning of the dangers beyond the safe boundaries of society. This may seem like harsh parenting, but it’s necessary. The alternative is far worse: retrieving only the remnants of a child in the morning or, more often, never finding them at all.

## **The Veil**

| *"The Veil is no mere fog, it is the breath of the unknown, a boundary between the world we know and a realm we are not meant to understand. Those who venture into it do not return because they are claimed by mysteries beyond our grasp."* — *Captain Rivleam of Aquelos, Navigator of the Carron* |
| :---- |

	  
The Veil is a dense, impenetrable mist that blankets the oceans of Lunaris, encircling the entire known world. Its origins and contents are a mystery; no one who has ventured into the Veil has ever returned to tell the tale. Since the dawn of recorded history, the Veil has remained an immutable presence, an ever-present boundary that Lunarians have long accepted as one of the many constants of their world. Attempts to shift, dissipate, or pierce the fog have all failed, reinforcing the belief that the Veil is an immovable force beyond human understanding.

## **Magic**

| *"Magic is a fickle companion, choosing its bearers with the same capriciousness as the winds. To wield it is to court the unknown, for it is less a gift and more a force that bends to none but its own will."* — *Veril Galadhel of Athia, Priestess of the Temple of True Winds* |
| :---- |

Magic in Lunaris is a rare and mysterious force, not commonly wielded by its inhabitants. It is perceived as a sentient, calculating entity that bestows its power selectively. Since the fall of the Age of Arcana, magic has been approached with extreme caution and fear. Its effects on reality are often lingering, leaving those who wield it with an overwhelming sense of responsibility.

*How the following nations approach magic:*

* Magic in Gracehold is tightly controlled by the crown and nobility. Only a select few Graces (citizens of Gracehold) are permitted to practice magic, regardless of whether they have been chosen by it or not. Unauthorized use of magic is considered treason, punishable by execution. The crown’s grip on magical practice reflects the deep-seated fear and mistrust that pervades the realm.  
* In the **Agrash Alliances**, magic is approached through a shamanistic lens. The  movements of the moon guide how magic should be wielded, with shamans interpreting these celestial signs. Without the moon’s guidance, delving into the unknown depths of magic is seen as a dishonorable and dangerous act, bringing peril not only to the caster but to all who are near.  
* In **Eldarel**, magic is confined to areas overseen by the Four Keepers of the land. Rituals are conducted in their honor to ensure that magic can be practiced safely. Any breach of this sacred pact is met with the wrath of the Ildarim, who are granted free reign to punish those who violate the sanctity of magic.  
* The land of **Zaldera** controls their magic through the Dragon Monarchs, literal gods that walk upon their regions. These Dragon Monarchs select bloodlines deserving sorcery and only those get to possess magic. In their lands primal magic is a nonexistence while arcane magic and divine magic are synonymous. Sorcery is the only form of means people cast magic in Zaldera, apart from pact magic for those desperate.

**Arcane Magic**  
Wizardry and sorcery have a different distinction in Lunaris. Both a wizard and a sorcerer possess an innate connection to magic itself but wizards possess a comparatively miniscule amount that need to be tailored and honed to create desirable effects while sorcerers are fonts of magic themselves that links their magical strength to personal growth rather than study. Sorcery rarely shows itself to those who are not under a special bloodline or to those who have not received a gift from magical beings. In extremely rare circumstances though it has happened to occur in new bloodlines. 

**Divine Magic**  
Divine magic is exceedingly rare in Lunaris, especially after the Fracturing. No longer granted simply through faith, divine power now requires the conscious favor of a god, a favor that is notoriously difficult to obtain. Both champions and clerics must earn their divine magic through exceptional faith or sheer luck, making such power a rare and precious commodity.

**Primal Magic**  
Primal magic is a gift bestowed by nature, granted only to those deemed worthy as nature’s warriors. The criteria by which nature chooses its wielders are arbitrary and inscrutable, though the Wilderkin seem to receive this gift more frequently than others almost to a degree of exclusivity.

Of course there are means to obtain magic for those who are truly desperate. Pact magic is an option for those who seek power and are willing to make the necessary sacrifices for it. 

### **Nexuses of Magic**

While magic is drawn through the primal, divine, occult, or arcane sources of the realm, for a spell to bend, shift, twist, or break structured reality a spellcaster must use an essence of reality to enforce their will to existence. Each spell must be drawn from a caster’s personal connection to an essence of reality, their nexus.

Mages often draw from multiple nexuses, with one acting as their main source. This mixed draw offers versatility, allowing them to adapt their magic to various needs and situations, though it tempers raw power with balance.

A primary draw, where one nexus dominates, marks a higher mastery. Mages who achieve this can channel the pure essence of that nexus, crafting spells of immense focus and potency.

The elusive unary draw,  to a single, undiluted nexus, is a legendary ideal, believed to be beyond mortal reach. Such a feat would channel pure, undivided essence, granting the caster unparalleled power.

*Each nexus connects to one of these essences and are listed as follows:*

* **Mandatum**  
  - The nexus of willful imposition, where the caster bends the fabric of reality through the force of structure and discipline. Those who connect with Mandatum see the world as a series of rules and forms waiting to be harnessed and redirected. By asserting order, they compel reality to adhere to their desires, like a sculptor shaping clay. Mandatum is about finding the underlying patterns in existence and reinforcing them to create something new. It is the belief that through discipline, structure, and unwavering focus, one can command reality to conform to their will.  
* **Cosmara**  
  - The nexus of reason and clarity, where the caster uses their understanding of reality’s intricacies to manipulate it. Casters who draw on Cosmara approach the world as a puzzle to be solved, using insight and logic to guide their magic. They perceive reality as a complex web of relationships and interactions, which can be carefully adjusted to achieve specific outcomes. In Cosmara, magic is a precise and deliberate act, where every decision is made with careful consideration. It is the belief that through knowledge, strategy, and analytical thinking, one can subtly reshape reality to their intent.  
* **Lyranthi**  
  - The nexus of inner resonance, where the caster taps into their deepest emotions to alter reality. This nexus is fueled by the belief that feelings are not just reactions to the world but powerful forces that can redefine it. Casters who embrace Lyranthi see reality as something that responds to the intensity of their inner life, whether it be passion, love, fear, or sorrow. In Lyranthi, magic is an extension of the self, where the caster’s emotions ripple outward to create tangible changes. It is the belief that by aligning one’s emotions with their intent, reality can be swayed by the sheer force of personal truth.  
* **Anarsyn**  
  - The nexus of flux and potential, where the caster harnesses the inherent unpredictability of existence to shape reality. Anarsyn is not about imposing control but about navigating the ever-shifting currents of possibility. Casters who channel Anarsyn embrace uncertainty, using the fluidity of reality to their advantage. They see the world as a place of boundless opportunities, where nothing is fixed, and everything is subject to change. In Anarsyn, magic is a dance with the unknown, where the caster’s will guides the chaotic energies of the universe to manifest their desires. It is the belief that by riding the waves of chaos, one can unlock the infinite potential within reality and bring forth new and unforeseen outcomes.
